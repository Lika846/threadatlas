# M5.2 plan: semantic search

Status: **approved 2026-11-12 (all three decisions), implemented and committed 2026-11-13.** Notes at the end. Builds on
ADR 0002 (decision 7, the fusion rule, still *proposed*), Experiment 2, and the committed
M5.1b index (`cc84b88`). No new architecture: the query is embedded by the same inference
child process, and vectors are read through the existing `openVectorReader`.

## Scope

Search conversations by meaning on the existing Search page, with useful results:
- the conversation title;
- the passage that matched;
- a link to that message;
- why it matched.

Nothing else. Out of scope: related conversations, themes, the graph, a larger model,
filters, saved searches, `sqlite-vec` or any other vector index.

## How it works

1. **Two modes on the Search page:**
   - **By meaning** is the default when the index is usable.
   - **Exact words** is today's keyword search, unchanged.
2. **Query embedding:** the query goes to an inference child (`embedQuery`, the `query: `
   prefix), as a code-only IPC request like indexing's.
3. **Vector scoring:** brute force over an in-memory int8 cache, filled through
   `openVectorReader`.
   - The cache is reloaded only when the index's `epoch` or `changeCounter` changes.
   - It holds one packed buffer, about 0.4 KB per chunk (20 MB at 50,000 chunks).
   - A conversation's score is its best chunk's score; the top 50 conversations are kept.
   - Measured in Experiment 2: 37 ms p50 at 50,000 chunks.
4. **Fusion (ADR 0002 decision 7, as measured):**
   - Semantic results lead.
   - The exact-word results are fused by reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60) only when that
     query returns anything; otherwise the results are semantic only.
   - Experiment 2: gated fusion never scored below semantic-only (0.83 / 0.83 / 0.71),
     while plain fusion hurt cross-language results.
   - Today's keyword search already requires every word, so it *is* the exact-word list.
     It is reused as is.
5. **Results** reuse the keyword result view, paged 20 at a time:
   - title and date;
   - **the passage:** the best chunk, read from the message by its offsets, shown as plain
     text. When the conversation also matched the words, the keyword snippet with its
     highlights is shown instead;
   - **a link to that message;**
   - **a label:** "Similar meaning", "Matches your words" or both.
6. **When meaning search can't run**, the page uses exact words and says why in one static
   line. The cases: model not installed, index empty, incompatible or still being built.
   For a partial index: "N conversations aren't indexed yet; they appear only in exact-word
   results", with a link to Settings.
7. **Stale vectors are never shown:** the reader already joins on the current
   conversation version and epoch (I6), and the cache follows `changeCounter`.

## The one new runtime piece: a warm query process

Embedding a query needs the model. Starting it takes 1.2–1.6 s plus the SHA-256 check,
too slow for every query.

**Proposed:**
- One **query child** per server process, on `globalThis`, started by the first meaning
  search.
- It stays loaded while searches continue and exits after **5 minutes idle**, so about
  0.8 GB is held only while you search.
- The first search after idle takes about 2 s; the page says "Loading the search model…".
- Queries are serialised, one request in flight as today; the page's latest-wins rule
  already drops older results.
- It is the same `InferenceProcess`, entry, drain, watchdogs and test seam as indexing. It
  is separate from the indexing child, so searching during indexing works, at about 1.6 GB
  together.

## Decisions requested

1. **Approve ADR 0002 decision 7** (gated RRF, k = 60) as the *By meaning* behaviour, with
   *Exact words* kept as a separate mode.
2. **Approve the warm query child** with a 5-minute idle exit. The alternative, a child
   per query, costs about 2 s per search.
3. **Top 50 conversations by meaning per query**, fused with the exact-word results, then
   paged 20 at a time.

## Quality fix: very short chunks (2026-11-13, approved and committed)

**Report:** in real use, "some recipe" did not find a conversation about a dinner idea, and
"when i was debugging some code" did not find a Python debugging conversation, although
"debugging" and "python" did.

**Investigation** (synthetic reproduction only: `tests/helpers/chat-corpus.ts`, real model;
no real data was read):
- **Ruled out:**
  - indexing: the targets were indexed;
  - prefixes: `query: ` and `passage: ` are correct;
  - filtering: nothing excluded the targets;
  - the gate: neither query matched all its words anywhere, so the results were by
    meaning only;
  - chunking: each target's best chunk was a sensible passage.
- **The cause:** chatter chunks.
  - **Real conversations are full of acknowledgements** ("ok", "thanks!", "works,
    thanks"), and their embeddings sit near the centre of the space. For any short query
    they score as high as a real answer: for "recipe", "thank you" scored 0.8539 against
    the dinner answer's 0.8536.
  - **Every conversation has such chunks,** and a conversation is scored by its best chunk.
    So they crowd out relevant conversations, and the top result's "passage" can be
    "thanks!".
  - **Measured in a synthetic archive of 334 conversations** (58% of its chunks under 6
    tokens): "recipe" found the dinner conversation at rank 8 and "debugging" found the
    Python conversation at rank 11.
  - **Why exact-word queries still worked:** fusion with the exact-word results lifted
    their conversation over the chatter.
  - **Why the research sets missed it:** they contain no chatter (12 short chunks of
    308).
- **Alternatives measured** (semantic only):

  | Option | v0 nDCG@10 | long nDCG@10 | Fixes the misses |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Current | 0.817 | 0.828 | no |
  | Ignore chunks under 4 / 6 / 8 tokens | 0.817 / 0.819 / **0.840** | 0.828 | yes |
  | Centre the vectors on the corpus mean | 0.755 | 0.852 | yes |

**The fix** (`vector-cache.ts`, query time only):
- A chunk under `MIN_SEARCH_TOKENS` = 8 model tokens counts only when its conversation has
  no longer chunk.
- Such conversations still appear, after every conversation with a longer match.
- The reader now also returns each chunk's stored `tokenCount`.
- No reindex, schema, model or dependency change.

**Before → after** (the full search path, real model):

| Query | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "recipe" | rank 8; top "passage" a 9-character acknowledgement | rank 1 |
| "debugging" | rank 11; top "passage" "thanks!" | rank 1 |
| "some recipe", "something to cook tonight", "when i was debugging some code", "fixing an error in my script", "python" | rank 1 | rank 1 |
| Research set v0, nDCG@10 (fused) | 0.826 | 0.840 |
| Research set long, nDCG@10 (fused) | 0.828 | 0.828 |
| Warm search, p95 | — | 11–13 ms |

The two reported queries already ranked first in the reproduction, so the mechanism was
reproduced on their close variants; the user's archive was not inspected.

**Regression tests:**
- **Real model:** `meaning-search.model.test.ts` checks the 7 natural-language queries in
  the 334-conversation archive: each must rank first, with a real passage. With the fix
  disabled it fails ("recipe" 8, "debugging" 11).
- **Unit:** demotion, the fallback for conversations with only short chunks, and the
  threshold boundary.

**Limitation:** a conversation whose only meaningful message is very short (under about
5–6 words, for example "What is a monad?") with a generic reply ranks after conversations
with longer matches for that query.

## Relevance cutoff (2026-11-13, approved and committed)

Approved: a 0.03 relative-score margin; exact-word matches always visible; the ranking and
the 50-result limit preserved; the rest under "Other results", expanded on the same page
without searching again.

**Implementation:**
- **Close matches** (`meaning-search.ts`, `closeMatches`, `CLOSE_MATCH_MARGIN = 0.03`):
  every exact-word match, and every meaning match within 0.03 of the best one.
- **Like with like:** a conversation scored only by very short chunks is never close to a
  best match that has a longer chunk. Otherwise the chatter demoted by the earlier quality
  fix would come back as close matches; an integration test found this.
- **One response:** a search by meaning now returns all its results (at most 50) at once,
  each flagged `close`. By meaning no longer pages; exact words still does.
- **The page** lists the close matches, then an "Other results (N)" button
  (`aria-expanded`) that reveals the rest in the same order. Expanding sends no request
  (e2e).

**Results** (real model):

| Set | Relevant among close matches | Close matches shown on average | nDCG@10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| research v0 | 79.6% | 5.8 | 0.840, unchanged |
| research long | 90.5% | 3.3 | 0.828, unchanged |

In the synthetic chat archive every target ranks first. Close matches number 1–5 for six
searches and 50 for "debugging", the broad-query limit: all 50 conversations are within the
margin.

**Tests:**
- **Unit:** the rule, exact-word matches, and short-only conversations.
- **Integration:** one response, flags, and close matches as an unbroken run at the top.
- **Model:** at least 75% of relevant conversations among close matches, at most 8 shown on
  average.
- **E2E:** close matches, "Other results" opening without a request, no pager.

The owner's archive is not in the repository.
