=== t217 | Learning Norwegian as an adult | languages | - | en
U: Moved to Bergen six months ago and my Norwegian is stuck at "takk" and "hei". How do I actually learn it?

=== t219 | Как выучить английские времена | languages | - | ru
U: Не могу запомнить, когда Present Perfect, а когда Past Simple. Есть простое правило?
A: Главный вопрос: важно ли, когда именно это случилось?
- Past Simple — когда есть конкретное время в прошлом или оно понятно из контекста: I saw him yesterday. I lived in Paris in 2019.
- Present Perfect — когда важен результат сейчас или опыт без указания времени: I have lost my keys (их нет сейчас). I have been to Paris (опыт).
Слова-подсказки: yesterday, last year, ago → Past Simple; already, yet, ever, never, just, since, for → чаще Present Perfect. Американцы употребляют Present Perfect реже британцев, так что в разговорной речи ошибки здесь не критичны.

=== t222 | Shadowing technique | languages | - | en
U: Someone recommended "shadowing" for pronunciation. What is it?
A: You listen to a short recording of a native speaker and repeat it at the same time, or a split second behind, copying rhythm, intonation and sounds — not just the words. Use clips of 1–2 minutes with a transcript, repeat each one many times over a week, and record yourself to compare. It's especially good for the melody of a language, which textbooks don't teach.

=== t224 | French subjunctive | languages | - | en
U: when do i need the subjunctive in french, i always get it wrong after "il faut que"
A: After "il faut que" it's always subjunctive: il faut que tu viennes, il faut que je fasse. The main triggers are expressions of necessity, wish, emotion and doubt followed by "que": vouloir que, être content que, douter que, avant que, pour que, bien que. After "après que" and "espérer que" you use the indicative — those two catch everyone.

=== t225 | Exam stress and procrastination | exams | stress | en

=== t228 | Memorising anatomy | exams | - | en
U: First year medicine, drowning in anatomy terms. Tips?
A: Active recall beats rereading by a lot. Turn lectures into questions (flashcards or blank diagrams to label), test yourself daily with spaced repetition, and draw structures from memory rather than copying. === t231 | Thesis outline in marine biology | thesis | - | en
U: My master's thesis is on microplastics in mussels along the coast. My supervisor says my outline is "unfocused". Help?
A: "Unfocused" usually means there are several research questions competing. Pick one main question you can answer with your data, for example: "Does microplastic concentration in blue mussels differ between harbour and non-harbour sites?" Then structure the outline around it: background that leads only to that question, methods that answer it, results in the order the question needs, and a discussion that comes back to it. Move interesting side findings to one short section or an appendix.
U: I also had a whole chapter about policy
A: Keep a short policy paragraph in the discussion — what your results mean for monitoring — and drop the chapter. A thesis that answers one question well is much stronger than one that touches five.
